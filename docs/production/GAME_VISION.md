# Game vision

**LOST FREQUENCY** is a first-person exploration game in which a wanderer crosses the levels of
the Backrooms exactly as one wiki continuity documents them. The fantasy is not combat or
crafting. It is *being lost in a place with rules*: noticing the wrongness, learning the
documented ways out, and surviving the hazards the pages describe.

## Pillars
1. **Source-locked places.** Each level is recognisably its page: materials, light, sound, scale
   and the defining anomaly. Entities and exits come only from the page.
2. **Exits are knowledge.** Progress comes from discovering documented routes (a flickering wall,
   a broken door, an arcade cabinet), cued by sound, light and architecture, never by glowing
   portals. The journal records only routes you actually used.
3. **Rules as systems.** Peripheral Shift, darkness, depth and oxygen, heat, sealing rooms: each
   page's defining rule is a stateful mechanic, not a sign on a wall.
4. **Honest edges.** Unbuilt destinations are named as unbuilt. Developer tools are separate and
   labelled. Story saves are never touched by debug actions.
5. **Respect quiet.** Empty, safe or tranquil levels stay that way. Absence is designed, not
   unfinished.

## "Minecraft standards" without Minecraft
The user's bar means density of discovery, traversal and replayability: seeded worlds that
differ across seeds, real physical movement (stairs, drops, swimming, climbing) and places that
reward looking around. It does not mean voxels, mining, mobs or a mandatory crafting loop.

## Diegetic vs out-of-story UI
In-world: notes, sounds, light, architecture and the journal of confirmed routes. Out-of-story:
pause menu, settings, credits and the developer browser. These sit outside canon, and the HUD
says so whenever developer mode is active.
