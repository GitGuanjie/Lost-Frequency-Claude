The stocky frame and head of curly blonde hair stirred after sixteen hours of deep sleep. Her head pushed into the plasticky but comfortable pillow first, then her limbs stretched and joints clicked. Finally, signifying the woman was fading back into consciousness, she groaned sleepily as her eyelids struggled open.
"Ngh.. Fuck…" The woman grumbled, folding an elbow to rub the sleep from her eyes and drool from her mouth. Though her vision was blinkered by locks of hair, she still made out the room she'd fallen asleep in. She was boxed inside a rectangular room by four, corporate-white walls, a dropped ceiling, and a fuzzy, carpeted grey floor. An office break room with added amenities. She'd been told as such when she was shown it last night.
With a heave of effort, the woman was able to sit upright and dangle her legs off the bed. She yawned and stretched more muscles, willing some energy she did not have. The room was hardly pristine, but in the roughly 30 waking minutes she'd spent in it, she'd already mucked it with her crusty clothes strewn over the carpet in a trail from the door to the pillow.
Though her legs still ached from the walking she'd done, she found the strength to get to her feet and shuffle across the carpet to the circular breakfast bar in the "living quarters". On it, folded up like they'd been pressed, was a new set of clothes for the woman to wear. As she picked the clothes up, as well as the shoes to their right, she noticed the top garment she was given.
A red sweater.
"Why not?! Why CAN'T I!? My friends are going!"
"And you're not. I'm not having you go off to some… ruddy fucking private school. No chance in hell. Not even like your fat ass would fit in a uniform, anyways."
"Oh, Ethan, huh? You really want to just jump ass first on the first guy that shows interest in you, huh? Is that it? You are your mother."
"Stop whining to me! I'm not letting you go off and get lofty, bright ideas. You're staying here . In Bristol. I'll teach you if I have to."
The woman shuddered as she walked with the clothes, and kept her head up as to not look at the jumper. As self conscious as she felt walking through an office in her current state, she passed through the other door she was told about last night and into a bathroom. Individual cubicles lined the left wall, with sinks embedded in a wall-to-wall countertop on the right. After she set her clothes down by the closest sink, she started to walk to the end stall.
Keeping her head turned minutely left to avoid the long mirror that followed the many sinks, the woman walked by the stalls to the last one. The slightly shoddy work on the ground inside the final stall was apparent before she even opened the door. The amateurish plastering, uneven implementation of debossed tiles, and the clear outline in the back wall where the toilet of the stall used to be all showed the hands of someone unskilled did this work. All done to accommodate the addition of a showerhead, which the woman quickly stepped under.
She closed the cubicle door and turned the hot water tap. Icy cold water sprayed in a narrow jet right onto her forehead, and though she did recoil and scrunch her face, she had no intentions of moving. The water ran down her front, spreading a chill from forehead to feet, but all she did was breathe to wait for the water to warm.
The feeling was not unlike tempting the water at the beach.
"Th-There it is, Jen! The Beach!"
"Yep! Gosh, you're excited…"
"I need to lock the car, silly…" … "Okay, then… Ah— No, don't go too far yet! You don't have a swimming costume!"
"You… don't? Huh. Suppose it sounds like your dad to raise a strange girl… Hey, no, I said wait up! Stop running!"
The woman didn't spend a second longer under the nostalgic water than she had to. When her body was suitably clean of the past few days, she turned the tap back and stepped out the shower. Though it took a moment, she quickly found where the towels were being kept—a crudely installed towel ring next to the hand dryers—and wrapped herself in two. One for her body, and one for her hair.
Leaving a trail of water in her wake, the woman walked across the length of the bathroom to her clothes. When she dried her body enough, doing such while facing away from the mirrors, she turned sideways and started to pick through the clothes. It only took a minute before she was clad in black trousers, a boxy shirt, and white sneakers. The red sweater, branded with a nonsensical company name on the back, she left crumpled on the countertop.
She fussed with her hair for a good fifteen minutes next, seemingly never comfortable with how dry she was able to get it. Even when it was rubbed enough to leave her scalp red. She gave up when her head stung like it was given a carpet burn, and looked about the room for any containers that might have a hairbrush.
"Ah." She sighed to herself, realising there were no such amenities. Though she pinched the bridge of her nose in frustration, it didn't take long for her to realise it was the least of her concerns.
Instead, she turned fully, and looked at herself in the closest mirror.
" God, you still look like shit. " She chastised to herself, the corners of her mouth turning in disgust. She pulled her bottom right eyelid down, looking at her bloodshot eyes and the dark circles that surrounded them. Her two fingers tapped down to her fatty cheeks, her narrow mouth, and the lardy padding under her skin that infected the rest of her body.
Using her fingers, as much as they were far too wide to work well as a brush, she started to iron out the knots in her hair. She winced at any little snag, and cursed either at herself or the pain each time. Though her eyes darted away from her reflection, her self in the mirror always tempted a glance back its way. More than once, her gaze returned to her body.
She felt like she was looking at herself in the water again.
"Am I…?" She asked weakly, still hacking up sand from her dry mouth. She felt the compulsion to clear her throat or cough constantly, and nearly spluttered on the grit she inhaled when she spoke again. She crawled away from her shallow grave towards the water, shaking her head and trying to forget what she saw, and caught her distorted reflection in the waves.
"Look at me…" She murmured, bringing her hands forwards to lean closer. The water finally made contact with her skin, licking her gently as the waves pulled back and forth. Whenever the water came in, she saw her face. She was soaked through, that much she could feel, and sand covered her face and stuck in her hair like glue. She noticed a bruise or two, too.
"How am I…?"
Her breathing heavy, she put her foot on the gas pedal and followed Bristol Road until she could no more. She passed the art gallery, watching curious people funnel in and out of the building, and parked the car in the lot designated for the historic lighthouse.
Her legs shaky, and her eyes still wet, she pulled herself out of the car once she parked in whatever empty space she could find. The crapbox spluttered before it stopped, but she wouldn't need to worry about it anymore. It was all somebody else's problem.
She passed the ticket machine for the car park, already getting a handful of eyes from the tourists who were surprised to watch the emotional, unkempt woman stumble towards the Atlantic Ocean. She stopped at the cliff's edge, and although she was several dozen meters above the ground, the ocean loudly lapped on the rocks below.
According to her phone, now sitting forever in the car, the tide was high but receding.
She'd finally be able to escape.
That fact felt more firm with every step closer to the precipice she took.
The woman snapped back to the present when the door out in the break room knocked loudly. Her manic, wide eyes returned to normal with some fast blinks. Her hands, tightly gripping the counter and her hair, unfurled and rested at her sides. Her back straightened so as to not be leaning over the sink.
"Hey! Are you, uh, awake and decent in there? Did you find the showers okay?" The voice, belonging to the same man from the night before, called through into the breakroom. His voice sounded practically underwater between two doors, but she heard him okay nonetheless.
"Uh, yes. Yes!" She replied, speaking louder when she realised the man wouldn't be able to hear her pitiful mumble. She looked back to the reflection, scowled at her teary face, and used the sweater to wipe her eyes before returning to the break room and opening the door.
"Hey, there you are." The man from the day before said, giving the woman a warm but simple smile. He looked a year-or-so younger than her, and wore a pretty simple work suit that looked incongruous with his gelled and trimmed black hair.
"Hi, yeah." The woman said, stifling a yawn but still moving her hand to cover it.
"Still sleepy?" He asked, changing which hand was responsible for carrying a pretty big looking briefcase. It was a shiny metal with a yellow tag on the handle, and would look industrial were it not for its pristineness.
"I… don't know. Have I been asleep for long…?"
"We got you into this room about, uh, sixteen hours ago?" When did you fall asleep?"
"…Sixteen hours ago. I've only been up for an hour." The woman told him guiltily, rubbing the back of her neck. She looked back into the room, mainly to yesterday's and the unmade bed, and seethed. Her guilt worsened when the man looked in with her. "Sorry about the mess, too. I'll clean it."
"We have janitors, don't worry. And… you know, you need sleep and to take things easy. Entering the Backrooms isn't ever light on anybody." The man explained, sighing afterwards and flexing his straining fingers on his briefcase. When the room went silent, the man opted to offer his free hand her way.
"My name's Alex, by the way. In case you don't remember from yesterday. It's nice to finally talk to you properly."
The woman looked down to his hand. Tentatively, and remembering what had happened the past week, she reached forwards and shook it.
"Holly." She said with her best attempt at a smile back.
